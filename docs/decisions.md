# Decisions

Where the docs contradict each other or leave something undefined, the build picks the reading most consistent with the Money rules and records it here. Entries marked **Open: owner** would change how money is counted if decided differently. The code follows the rules as written for those until the owner decides.

| # | Topic | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D-1 | Default branch | The repository's branch is `master` (the empty repo's default). Workflows trigger on `master`. Rename it to `main` in GitHub settings if you prefer; the workflows accept both. | The doc says `main`, but the connected repo started on `master`. |
| D-2 | Data access | Plain SQL migrations plus a thin typed data-access layer, not Drizzle. Every query goes through `budgetScoped()`, which requires and checks the budget id. | Same portability (SQLite SQL), no ORM on the hot path, nothing to keep in sync between a Drizzle schema and SQL files. |
| D-3 | Design export | `docs/design.md` is the verbatim Markdown export. The Architecture section's embedded diagram (a widget) can't be exported to Markdown, so the export keeps its placeholder line. | The instruction was not to edit the content. |
| D-4 | Plaid Sandbox | No Sandbox keys were available in the build environment, and the Plaid MCP can't issue them. The go/no-go ran against `tools/plaid-mock`, which serves Plaid's request and response shapes with Sandbox-sized transactions. The same harness runs against Sandbox when `PLAID_CLIENT_ID` and `PLAID_SECRET` are in `apps/worker/.dev.vars`. | Keeps the measurement honest and repeatable; Sandbox validation is listed in the manual steps. |
| D-5 | Raw page storage | Plaid's pages are stored once in R2 (`RAW` bucket): staging during a run and the permanent raw archive afterwards. `bank_event.raw` became `raw_ref` (page key + position), and a new append-only `bank_page` table records each applied page and its R2 checksum. | Phase 1: carrying raw JSON per event through D1 made the apply step's serialization exceed the CPU budget (measurements.md). It also keeps D1 far below its 500 MB limit. |
| D-6 | Page size | 100 transactions per `/transactions/sync` call (not 500 or 250), applied in 50-event steps. `SYNC_PAGE_SIZE` accepts 50, 100, 250 or 500. | Phase 1: 500 and 250 don't fit 10 ms of CPU; 100 with 50-event steps fits with margin, and keeps a 3,000-transaction backfill at 30 Plaid calls. |
| D-7 | Projection | `bank_txn` is built inside D1 from the events in the same batch (`INSERT … SELECT json_extract(payload, …)`). | Transactions cross the wire once; the SQL runs in D1, not in Worker CPU. |
| D-8 | `apply_seq` | `bank_event.id` equals `apply_seq`, taken from `apply_counter` inside the batch. | Replay order is a primary-key scan, with no extra index (every index costs a D1 row write per insert). |
| D-9 | Provider port | `syncTransactions()` is split into `fetchSyncPage()` (returns the raw bytes plus cursor and has_more) and `parsePage()`. | The fetch step stores bytes without parsing them; the apply step parses. Same normalized types otherwise. |
| D-10 | Rule 6 and card payment categories | Rule 6 ("for every category") includes card payment categories. A payment category that is negative at month end (the card was paid more than it held, for example a starting debt paid before you assigned to it) adds that amount to the month's uncovered cash overspending, and the category starts the next month at 0. | Literal reading of rule 6. Without it, a negative payment category would carry forward indefinitely while RTA stayed too high. |
| D-11 | **Open: owner.** Money arriving in a category after it was credit-overspent in the same month | Rules as written: the month-end split is credit = min(−available, unfunded card spending), cash = the rest. If a later cash refund or inflow brings the category back to zero or above, nothing is reset, the card's payment category stays short by the unfunded amount, and covering the card later takes that amount from RTA a second time. Example: Dining $0, $30 card purchase (unfunded), $40 cash refund to Dining later that month. Dining carries $10, the card is $30 short, and checking holds $40 more. The budget counts $30 less than you have. | Proposed fix: at month end, move the unfunded card spending the category's own money covered into that card's payment category (here $30), so the card is covered and nothing is counted twice. |
| D-12 | **Open: owner.** Refund of a purchase whose credit overspending was already forgiven | Rules as written (rule 3: the category gets the full refund). If the purchase was unfunded and its month has ended, the overspending was already forgiven as card debt, so the refund gives the category money that no cash backs. Example: October, $30 on the card with Dining at $0; Dining resets and the card is $30 short. November, the $30 is refunded: Dining +$30, the debt is gone, and the budget now counts $30 more than you have. The same happens with an unlinked refund on a card whose payment category holds less than the refund. | Proposed fix: the part of a refund that matches debt already forgiven goes back against that debt, not to the category. |
| D-13 | Card inflows to Ready to Assign (rewards statement credits) | The rules don't say what a statement credit does to the card's payment category. It now gives back `min(credit, max(P, 0))` from the payment category, as an unlinked refund does, while RTA gets the full credit. | Rule 7 (P matches the card's balance) and "no money is created". This is also the only reading under which your November numbers hold together: with the prototype's $24.10 "Points back" on Sapphire, RTA $981.90 and Sapphire exactly covered at $878.32 both hold only if the credit releases $24.10 from the payment category. |
| D-14 | Rule 10 assertion | Asserted as: cash and card working balances = available in every non-payment category + RTA + Waiting to match + unsorted transfer sides (net) + uncategorized amounts (signed) − K. K is unbudgeted card debt, tracked from four events: starting card debt (rule 8), minus assignments to payment categories, plus credit overspending forgiven at month end, minus negative payment balances moved to RTA (D-10). When every card is fully budgeted, K = 0 and this is rule 10 word for word. The November fixture asserts both forms. | Rule 10 as written cannot hold while a card carries debt that was never assigned (rule 8's starting debt, or credit overspending from an earlier month), so it would fail on correct budgets. |
| D-15 | Prototype: "Transfers to sort out" | Not part of the canonical November fixture. The prototype's unclear transfer is −$200 out of checking with +$200 candidates into Ally and Marcus, held aside. That is +$200 net, which your identity line has no term for. The prototype's registers include the −$200 but not the deposits, so the screen can't be reconciled with its own balances. A separate test adds the three rows and shows that rule 10 then needs the unsorted-transfer term (+$200) while RTA stays $981.90. | Your listed numbers are right without it; the prototype example is the inconsistent part. |
| D-16 | Prototype registers | The prototype's registers don't list all of November's spending (for example, Dining's $136.15 outside the named purchases, Electric's $53.70, Subscriptions' $64.45). The fixture puts that spending in checking. End-of-period balances match your numbers exactly; the running balances inside the checking register differ from the mockup's. | The listed numbers are the regression target; the register lines are illustrations. |
| D-17 | When assignments count | An assignment counts from the start of its month, so it can fund card purchases made earlier that month. Covering Dining's $18.25 therefore funds the Shake Shack purchase and makes Freedom's payment category Covered, as the prototype shows. | Funding is computed in date order with each month's assignments available from day 1. |
| D-18 | Low status | "Month-to-date spending running more than 30 points ahead of the month's elapsed share" uses spending share = spent ÷ (spent + available). | Matches the prototype (Personal care is the only Low category in November). |
| D-19 | Same-month refunds of unfunded purchases | A linked card refund in the same month as the purchase first cancels that purchase's unfunded part before the month-end credit/cash split. | Otherwise later cash overspending would be misclassified as credit overspending and forgiven. Defines the split; doesn't change any rule. |
| D-20 | Transfers to unlinked accounts | A transaction marked by hand as a transfer to or from an account that isn't linked (for example Venmo) is money leaving or entering the budget accounts with no category, so it comes out of or goes into Ready to Assign. | Keeps rule 10 true; the Transfer detection section allows marking a transfer by hand to an unlinked account but doesn't say where the money goes. |
| D-21 | Month summaries | Each month's category and card cells are stored together with that month's carry-forward state in one `month_snapshot` row (with a change hash), instead of one `month_category_summary` row per category per month. The Budget page reads one row per month. | Phase 1: a first-month edit would otherwise write 36 × 60 = 2,160 cell rows; now it writes about 36. |
| D-22 | Recompute chunking | A recompute handles at most 6 months per invocation. Edits within the last 6 months (current month included), and all bank syncs, finish before the response, as designed. An edit to an older month writes those 6 months, leaves the rest dirty, and starts the recompute Workflow, which continues 6 months per step. Until it finishes (a few seconds), the app shows Ready to Assign as "Updating". | Phase 1: one recompute of 36 months costs about 20 ms of CPU, about 0.6 ms per month of history. This is the one place the design's "before the page responds" can't hold on the free plan. |
| D-23 | Target rounding | Refill and Balance-by-date targets use even steps with the remainder in the period's last month: a refill's month k of n is due k × floor((A − S) ÷ n), and the last month takes the rest. | The Targets section's formula uses a ceiling, but its text says "the remainder goes to the last month of the period". The text was followed; it changes only suggested amounts, never how money is counted. |
